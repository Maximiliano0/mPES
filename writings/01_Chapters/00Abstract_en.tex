\begin{otherlanguage}{english}

This thesis compares reinforcement learning methods on a sequential
allocation problem with limited resources. A simulated pandemic is used
as the test scenario: cities are presented sequentially, each with an
initial severity, and an agent decides how many resources to allocate to
each one from a fixed budget. Unaddressed severity grows at every step,
so an insufficient allocation to a city is amplified in the following
steps.

Six agents were trained: two tabular methods (Q-Learning and Double
Q-Learning) and four neural networks (a deep Q-network, a recurrent
variant that processes the most recent states, an actor--critic method
and a causal Transformer that attends to the recent history). Six rules
for combining the networks into an ensemble were also evaluated; in all
of them each network is weighted by its confidence in the decision,
derived from Shannon entropy. All
models were evaluated on the same 64 sequences of cities under the
training conditions and in 21 scenarios with other severities or
lengths, three of them outside the training range.

The comparison metric is the normalised performance of each sequence,
where 0 represents no allocation and 1 the optimal allocation, computed
with knowledge of the full sequence. The analysis identifies the
Transformer as the best individual model, both under training
conditions and in the generalisation scenarios.

Among the ensembles, only the weighted ensemble outperforms the
individual Transformer, by a small margin, and the one that includes a
gate regulating the participation of the Transformer reaches an
equivalent performance, since it executes the Transformer's action in
almost every decision. The advantage of the weighted
ensemble is associated with a high temperature and a severity prior
rather than with confidence weighting. The ensembles that only average
or vote with weights derived from Shannon entropy fall below the
individual Transformer, so this confidence is not sufficient on its own
to outperform the best member in terms of average performance.

\par\vspace{0.5\baselineskip}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} reinforcement learning, sequential
allocation with limited resources, causal Transformer, model ensembles, Shannon entropy,
generalisation.
